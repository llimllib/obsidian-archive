An [article](https://anniemueller.com/posts/how-i-a-non-developer-read-the-tutorial-you-a-developer-wrote-for-me-a-beginner) is making the rounds about what it feels like to read a technical article when you don't understand the terminology
- Why do people feel OK putting a partially-assed project on github, but have a much higher bar for when they release a technical article?
- It's _fractally_ difficult to write technical tutorials and documentation
	- Everybody comes to it with a different skillset
	- You're writing for _everybody in the world_ speaking every language
	- Every single thing you write about 
	- Computers are so new that there's very little common baseline for everyone
	- we don't even know what the fields are yet!
	- There's little-to-no feedback
	- People feel very free to give harsh criticism
		- That leaves only the thickest-skinned authors able to write
	- The worst part for me is the complete lack of feedback
		- You chuck an article out into the ether, and either you hear _nothing_ or the feedback you get ranges from mildly critical to harshly critical
		- There's neither reason nor incentive to leave positive things about it. Who cares that you enjoyed the article 
- Let's try to create a world of constructive dialogue
